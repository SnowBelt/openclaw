# Benchmark Channel Production Workflow

Pattern Lab must adapt the mechanics of high-performing channels in its realm without copying their scripts, thumbnails, titles, edits, or brand systems.

Canonical target: **Bright Sun Films mystery + The B1M polish + Not Just Bikes clarity + Here Grows New York map proof.**

## Non-Negotiable Pattern Lab Formula

**One city. One strange visual clue. One source trail. One hidden system.**

Every long-form package must answer these before rendering:

1. What is the strange visual clue?
2. What source proves the clue is real?
3. What hidden city system does it reveal?
4. What viewer promise is made by the title and thumbnail?
5. Where is that promise paid off in the first 30 seconds?

## Series Families

Use one repeatable series family per video:

- The Map Changed
- Vanished
- Under the City
- One Building Explains
- Before the Cars
- The Street That Moved
- City Myths

Generic `History of Detroit` packaging is blocked. A city can be the subject, but the viewer buys the mystery, contradiction, proof object, or system.

## Thumbnail Rules

- Three candidates minimum.
- Photo-first, source-backed visual base.
- One dominant focal point.
- One city anchor.
- One proof object.
- 2-4 words.
- High contrast at phone size.
- No copied layout from another channel.
- No generic skyline-only thumbnail.
- No fake archival image.
- No AI-generated proof object.

## First 5 Seconds

The opening must show a real source/photo/map clue and state a contradiction before Pattern Lab branding. No logo-first open. No generic welcome. No topic lecture.

Approved open pattern:

1. Visual clue.
2. Contradiction.
3. Source proof.
4. City/system frame.

## Visual System

The edit must feel like a documentary, not a slide deck. Visuals should include a deliberate mix of:

- people and communities,
- buildings and landmarks,
- attractions and civic spaces,
- maps and documents,
- neighborhoods and street life,
- industry and workers,
- transit and infrastructure,
- aerials and skyline context,
- newspapers, ads, signage, or archival objects.

If narration mentions a person, group, landmark, factory, street, attraction, neighborhood, or system, OpenClaw must prefer a relevant real visual. If a relevant visual is missing, OpenClaw must source more rights-safe media or use a clearly labeled non-proof illustration; it must not use unrelated filler.

## Shorts System

Every long-form episode must produce a 5-7 Short concept pack. The first package may render the best 3, but OpenClaw must know the full concept set so future cutdowns are not afterthoughts.

Each Short must be a standalone clue, not a trailer:

- first-frame promise,
- one visual clue,
- one proof/payoff,
- one viewer question or comment prompt,
- related-video bridge back to the long-form city file.

## Testing And Learning

When public testing is available, test up to three title/thumbnail combinations and choose the winner by watch-time share, CTR by traffic surface, first-30-second retention, and expectation-match comments.

After publishing, the learning loop must track:

- Home/Suggested CTR,
- Search CTR,
- first-30-second retention,
- average view duration,
- average percentage viewed,
- subscribers per 1,000 views,
- Shorts viewed/swiped behavior,
- Shorts-to-long clicks,
- city requests,
- local corrections,
- source disputes,
- which visual categories produced retention spikes or dips.

## Safety Boundary

Benchmarking means copying mechanics, not creative work. Do not copy another creator's thumbnail layout, wording, script structure, host style, music bed, pacing fingerprint, or distinctive design system.
