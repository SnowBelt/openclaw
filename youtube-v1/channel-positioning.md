# Pattern Lab Channel Positioning

Pattern Lab explains the hidden systems behind American cities.

The channel is a source-first city-history media property. It is not a generic history channel, trivia channel, travel vlog, creator-growth channel, AI-tools channel, or academic lecture series.

## Core Promise

- One city.
- One mystery.
- One source trail.
- The map keeps receipts.

Primary tagline: `Hidden systems behind American cities.`

Secondary line: `City. Source. System.`

Subscriber promise: `Subscribe for the next city file.`

## Target Market

Pattern Lab is built for city-curious adults who like history, maps, old photos, infrastructure, lost neighborhoods, industry, buildings, music, migration, and why-place-became-this-way stories.

High-value audience segments:

- Local and diaspora viewers with personal ties to the featured city.
- Urbanism and infrastructure viewers interested in roads, rail, zoning, downtowns, suburbs, freeways, transit, and civic systems.
- American history viewers interested in migration, labor, industry, civil rights, housing, music, culture, and policy consequences.
- Nostalgia and old-photo viewers drawn to then-now evidence and vanished places.
- Travel and city-curious viewers who want to understand a place before or after visiting.

## Audience Avatar

Morgan, 42, watches YouTube at night and likes videos that make places make sense. Morgan clicks titles like:

- `Why Detroit Looks Like This`
- `The Neighborhood Detroit Erased`
- `The Map That Changed Chicago`
- `How One Train Station Explains a City`

Morgan dislikes generic AI narration, random trivia, fake archival photos, and low-effort slideshows. Morgan subscribes when the channel helps them understand places better than before.

Subscriber trigger: `I know this city, but I did not understand it until now.`

## Value Proposition

Pattern Lab gives viewers a better way to understand cities. Each episode starts with a visual mystery, follows a source trail, and explains the system underneath the familiar story.

The channel should feel like a premium city-mystery, map-proof, hidden-systems show. It should not feel academic, generic, automated, or decorative.

## Content Pillars

- City Files.
- Lost Neighborhoods.
- Maps That Changed Cities.
- One Building Explains a City.
- People Who Built the City.
- Myth vs Source.
- Then/Now Evidence.

## Detroit Pilot Season

Detroit is the pilot city season. Treat it as a season, not one isolated upload.

Recommended first six long-form episodes:

1. `Detroit Didn't Just Decline. It Was Rewired.`
2. `The Neighborhood Detroit Erased.`
3. `How Detroit Became the Motor City.`
4. `The Freeway That Cut Through Detroit's Memory.`
5. `How Motown Changed America From One City Block.`
6. `The Station That Explains Detroit's Rise, Fall, and Comeback.`

## Profit Model

Pattern Lab should be built as a niche media property, not only an ad-supported channel.

Profit stack:

1. YouTube ads after YPP.
2. Sponsorships from map tools, history books, genealogy tools, travel/history platforms, archive/database tools, and carefully disclosed local museums or tours.
3. Affiliate revenue from books, maps, documentaries, and city-history resources.
4. Digital products after audience proof: printable city maps, city-file PDFs, Detroit source guides, and lost-neighborhood map packs.
5. Membership later: source-room access, vote on the next city, extended source notes, and behind-the-scenes research files.

## Launch KPI Targets

Long-form early targets:

- CTR: 4-8%.
- First 30-second retention: 65%+.
- Average percentage viewed: 40%+.
- Average view duration: 5+ minutes on a 12-minute video.
- Subscriber conversion: 5-15 subscribers per 1,000 views.

Shorts early targets:

- Viewed vs swiped: 60%+.
- Average percentage viewed: 80%+.
- Related-video clicks tracked aggressively.
- Subscriber conversion matters more than raw Shorts views.

## Banned Angles

Do not produce or package active Pattern Lab videos as:

- generic city trivia
- unsourced nostalgia
- fake archival scenes
- outrage-first civic commentary
- active crime coverage
- partisan political argument
- recycled documentary summaries
- travel ranking videos
- broad school-lecture overviews
- decorative old-photo slideshows

Historical images are evidence. AI visuals are graphics or reconstructions, never fake archival proof.
