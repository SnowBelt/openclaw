# Pattern Lab Long-Form Strategy

Pattern Lab long-form videos are the main watch-hour engine. The retooled channel studies American cities through maps, archives, photographs, neighborhoods, buildings, industries, and the decisions that still shape daily life.

## Strategy

- Optimize primarily for valid public long-form watch hours and subscriber conversion.
- Start with Detroit, then repeat the city-file format across other American cities.
- Use Shorts for discovery, but make long-form the place where trust, source depth, and watch time are earned.
- Keep the default runtime between 10 and 14 minutes until analytics justify longer episodes.
- Open with the result, source artifact, map, historical photo, or contradiction before any channel branding.
- Show proof in the first 20 seconds.
- Keep the Pattern Lab intro brief: identify James and the channel only after the hook earns attention.
- Use one clear source artifact per video: map, source ledger, historical photo board, timeline, building record, neighborhood overlay, route map, or then/now evidence board.
- Build the main body around source, place, date, visible clue, historical meaning, what changed afterward, and the final pattern.
- Include one light-touch James moment when it naturally supports the topic: a dry observation, recurring line, tiny preference, or Scout aside attached to the evidence.
- Change visuals only when the narration changes source, place, time period, proof state, example, or decision.
- End with the city-file outro: "That is the pattern: city, source, system." Then give one earned subscribe CTA.
- Never use broad unsourced summaries, random Google Images, fake archival AI, generic AI narration, copied thumbnails, unsupported money claims, or mass-produced templates.

## Practical Considerations

- Render 1920x1080 horizontal video with an audio stream.
- Voiceover target: natural adult narration, no cloned or celebrity-like voice, phone-speaker review required.
- Visual beat target: source/proof clip first, then script-matched beats that typically change every 5-14 seconds.
- Prefer verified historical photos, maps, newspapers, records, and public-domain/clear-license material.
- Use high-quality free stock video for context, pacing, and atmosphere only when the clip is rights-logged, watermark-free, and compatible with YouTube/commercial use.
- Preferred free B-roll sources: Pexels, Pixabay, Mixkit, Coverr, and selected Videvo/Videezy clips with item-level license review.
- Preferred archival video sources: National Archives, NASA, Internet Archive/Prelinger, and local archives when item-level rights are clear.
- AI visuals may fill gaps only as labeled graphics or reconstructions; never present them as archival evidence.
- Every real historical image, archival video, stock clip, and generated graphic needs a rights-ledger row with title, source URL, archive/platform, creator when known, year when relevant, rights basis, attribution text, commercial-use status, modification status, and local path.
- James personality should appear through judgment and taste, not biography. Keep personal moments under 5-8 seconds.
- Approved James lines include "Show me the source.", "That is a vibe. It is not evidence.", "Useful, but not magic.", and "The map keeps receipts."
- Scout can be mentioned occasionally, but never as a recurring gimmick.
- Metadata must include 5 title options, a default title-thumbnail pair, description, tags, chapters, pinned comment, synthetic-disclosure decision, and subscribe CTA.
- Thumbnail/title must match the proof shown in the first 30 seconds.
- Use YouTube retention data after publish: first 30 seconds, top moments, spikes, dips, average view duration, watch hours, and subscriber conversion.
- If CTR is weak but retention is strong, repackage title and thumbnail.
- If CTR is strong but retention is weak, revise the hook, proof timing, visual pacing, or promise match.

## Long-Form Quality Bar

The Long-Form agent must block a draft when any of these are true:

- Runtime is under 10 minutes or over 14 minutes unless explicitly approved by the owner for a dense or experimental cut.
- Proof does not appear first or is not represented in the first 20 seconds.
- The script opens with generic filler before the hook.
- The speaker/channel intro is missing or too late.
- The outro is missing the city-file signoff.
- The subscribe CTA is missing or appears before value is delivered.
- The script has no approved James persona moment, overuses persona moments, or invents unverifiable biography.
- The video lacks a 1920x1080 render or audio stream.
- The visual beat plan is missing, generic, or not matched to narration excerpts.
- Metadata is missing title options, chapters, pinned comment, subscribe CTA, or synthetic-disclosure decision.
- Rights ledger rows are missing for historical images, voiceover, proof footage, and the long-form video.
- Stock footage is watermarked, editorial-only, unlogged, visually generic, or used as if it were historical proof.
- The draft feels like a slideshow, a template, a scraped summary, a fake archival reconstruction, or generic AI output.

## Optimization Targets

- Primary: public watch hours, average view duration, average percentage viewed, first 30-second retention, subscriber conversion.
- Secondary: impressions click-through rate, suggested/browse traffic, comments quality, returning viewers, city requests.
- Monetization safety: original narration, source-backed claims, clear commentary, advertiser-friendly historical framing, no artificial engagement, no reused or inauthentic content signals.
