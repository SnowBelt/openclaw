# Pattern Lab Brand Kit

## Visual Direction

Aesthetic: archival civic investigation.

Pattern Lab should feel like a premium city-history evidence room: source-first, local, warm, restrained, and visually clear on a phone. It should not look like cyberpunk, glossy AI, a dashboard, a generic slideshow, or a loud social-video template.

Brand promise: one city, one mystery, one source trail. The map keeps receipts.

## Official Color System

- Deep charcoal `#111315`: primary background and cinematic negative space.
- Ink panel `#1B1A17`: overlay/caption cards.
- Archival paper `#E8DDC5`: evidence cards, map labels, and source inserts.
- Old map blue `#2E5E73`: neighborhood outlines, map panels, and civic system graphics.
- Evidence amber `#C88A2D`: proof markers, route lines, and payoff highlights.
- Copper/rust `#9B4F2F`: secondary historical emphasis.
- Caption white `#F4F0E8`: primary caption text.
- Proof red `#B23A2E`: displacement/cut markers, used sparingly.
- Muted archive gray `#AFA58F`: supporting labels and low-priority metadata.

## Official Typography

Free-font direction:

- Hook/headline: Archivo Black.
- Caption/body: Inter.
- Source labels: IBM Plex Mono.

Local renderer fallbacks when Google Fonts are not installed:

- Hook/headline fallback: Avenir Next Condensed Heavy.
- Caption/body fallback: Avenir Next.
- Source-label fallback: SF Mono.

Avoid decorative vintage fonts, generic meme fonts, neon gamer fonts, and unreadable condensed text. Uppercase is reserved for 2-5 word hooks and proof labels.

## Shorts Style

Every Short must be a standalone micro-story, not a random clip.

Required arc:

1. First-frame proof hook: city/neighborhood + proof object + 2-5 words.
2. Hook: one clear claim.
3. Evidence: map, archival photo, source, document, street, business, or route.
4. Payoff: what the proof changes.
5. Bridge: full city file / source trail.

Visual pacing target: a meaningful visual event every 1.5-3 seconds.

## Thumbnail Style

Thumbnails must be bold, phone-readable, honest, and tied to a first-30-second payoff.

Preferred visual tensions:

- A map line cutting through a city.
- A vanished neighborhood outline.
- An old photo beside a modern void.
- A familiar building used as evidence.
- A source document that challenges a familiar city myth.

Every thumbnail must include:

- City name.
- One strong proof object.
- 2-4 words of hook text.

Blocked thumbnail patterns:

- Generic skyline-only thumbnails.
- Unexplained boxes, arrows, lines, or filler props.
- Pure mystery without visible source payoff.

## End Screen

Default end screen text: `Subscribe for the next city file.`

End screen visual: map grid, archive frame, source marker, and small Pattern Lab mark.

## AI Visual Rule

AI visuals may be used as graphics, diagrams, or clearly labeled reconstructions. They must never be presented as archival photographs or historical proof.

## Stock And Archival Media Rule

Use free high-quality stock video and photos to make episodes feel cinematic, but keep them subordinate to source proof. Stock footage is context, atmosphere, pacing, or transition material. Historical photos, archival footage, maps, records, and original Pattern Lab graphics carry the argument.

Every stock, archival, generated, or original media asset used in final output needs a rights-ledger row before owner review. Do not use watermarked previews, editorial-only clips in monetized uploads, unclear-license footage, or recognizable trademarks/properties without explicit clearance.
