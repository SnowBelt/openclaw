# Pattern Lab Shorts Strategy

Pattern Lab Shorts are discovery assets. Their job is to stop the swipe, prove one city-history idea quickly, and move the right viewer toward the full city file without becoming generic trivia.

## Strategy

- Cut Shorts from original long-form work only.
- Make the first frame a clear city-history promise, not a random cropped frame.
- Treat the first frame like the Shorts thumbnail/cover: it must read on a phone before the viewer hears a word.
- Use one idea per Short: curiosity, utility, or identity.
- Put the source/proof visual on screen before the viewer wonders whether the claim is real.
- Keep the default length between 30 and 60 seconds, with 25-45 seconds allowed for dense clips when the payoff lands faster.
- Burn in concise on-screen text because Shorts are often judged before audio earns attention.
- End every Short with a bridge to the long-form city file, a related-video checklist item, and a light subscribe cue.
- Optimize for viewed-versus-swiped-away, average percentage viewed, replays, subscriber conversion, and related-video clicks.
- Do not use third-party clips, claimed music, generic trivia, fake archival AI, or recycled city commentary.

## Practical Considerations

- Render vertical 1080x1920 video.
- Every Short needs a first-frame promise, hook, proof/payoff, related-video bridge, and subscribe cue.
- Keep caption lines short enough to read on a phone.
- Avoid over-captioning the whole screen; the source artifact still needs breathing room.
- Use high-contrast Pattern Lab styling, not platform-copying UI.
- Use no copyrighted music by default. If music is added later, use YouTube-safe sources and log it.
- Shorts can be up to three minutes on YouTube, but Pattern Lab defaults to 30-60 seconds because the monetization goal is discovery, subscriber conversion, and related-video clicks.
- Avoid Shorts over one minute unless there is a proven reason; claimed third-party content in longer Shorts creates avoidable monetization and availability risk.
- Judge the first frame and title as a pair: the text should create a reason to watch without repeating the title or making a promise the proof cannot satisfy.
- After publish, use the decision label:
  - double down
  - revise hook
  - improve crop
  - improve first-frame promise
  - retire moment
  - expand into series
  - spin off city series

## Pattern Lab Shorts Agent Role

The Shorts agent acts as a short-form marketer and editor. It owns:

- choosing the most compelling moments from the long-form city-history script
- assigning curiosity, utility, and identity psychology
- creating first-frame text
- enforcing a proof/payoff structure
- generating related-video bridge copy
- validating vertical format, length, captions, and rights ledger rows
- preserving the subscribe CTA without making the Short feel like an ad
- reading post-publish Shorts metrics and turning rejection reasons into better future cuts

The agent must block a Short when it is generic, too slow, badly cropped, missing a visible proof moment, missing a related-video bridge, missing the subscribe cue, or detached from the long-form city artifact.
