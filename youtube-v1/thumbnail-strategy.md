# Pattern Lab Thumbnail Strategy

Pattern Lab thumbnails are city-mystery packaging. Their job is to earn the right click from the right viewer, then make the first 30 seconds feel like the click was honest.

Canonical machine-readable policy: `youtube-v1/resources/thumbnail-click-policy.json`.

Production workflow: `youtube-v1/workflows/thumbnail-production-workflow.md`.

Core formula: one dominant real photo/map/document + dominant city name + one emotional mystery + one city anchor + one proof object + 2-4 words.

## Strategy

- Build the thumbnail before final upload metadata locks.
- Create five city-first review concepts for every long-form video, then select exactly three meaningfully different production candidates: emotional mystery, map/system proof, and contrarian history angle.
- Make the city name primary or co-primary text. The viewer must clearly see the city being discussed before reading the title.
- Pair every thumbnail with a specific city-history title promise.
- Use one dominant real visual: a person/crowd/action photo, map, old photo, lost place, building, route line, or source clue.
- Prefer people, workers, crowds, transit, street life, factories, attractions, landmarks, demolition, vanished places, or strong route/map proof over empty skyline/context images.
- Create curiosity through a visible city tension: what changed, what vanished, what the map reveals, what the familiar story missed, or what one place explains.
- Keep text short enough to read on a phone: ideally 2-4 words, never a sentence.
- Show or imply a real source category: map, archive photo, building record, newspaper, timeline, neighborhood outline, or then/now frame.
- Use at most one major proof mark: one route line, circle, arrow, tear line, or simple split. Do not add multiple boxes/lines that compete with the click question.
- Apply competitive thumbnail-grade contrast: yellow/white city text, dark edges, bright focal point, one bold accent color, thick stroke, and strong text shadow.
- Render a local YouTube-style thumbnail search shelf and fail candidates where the city name is a small tag, weak, or unreadable at reduced size.
- Make each candidate meaningfully different so YouTube Test & Compare can learn something.
- Optimize for watch-time share, first-30-second retention, and subscriber conversion, not raw CTR alone.
- Use YouTube A/B testing when available and choose winners by watch time first, not CTR alone.

## Required Thumbnail Families

Build five review concepts for every long-form video. Choose from these city-first families:

- `DETROIT WAS REWIRED`: city + contradiction.
- `DETROIT'S HIDDEN MAP`: city + map/system proof.
- `DETROIT 1942`: city + year/time-travel source clue.
- `DETROIT VANISHED`: city + vanished-place clue.
- `NOT JUST DECLINE`: documentary fall/rise contradiction with a large Detroit lockup.
- `THE MAP CHANGED`: map line, route, border, freeway, rail, annexation, or street-grid tension.
- `WHAT VANISHED`: old photo, vanished block, lost neighborhood, demolished building, or erased district outline.
- `NOT THE WHOLE STORY`: familiar city myth challenged by a source, map, or dated image.
- `THIS EXPLAINS DETROIT`: one place, building, street, or source clue that unlocks the city story.
- `BEFORE THE CARS`: pre-auto Detroit or pre-familiar-story visual contrast.
- `CITY FILE`: broad channel-branded package when the episode needs a series identity rather than a specific object.

## Practical Considerations

- Required upload file: 16:9 PNG, at least 1280x720, preferably 1920x1080 for source quality.
- Preferred working canvas: 3840x2160, then export a 1920x1080 or YouTube-ready 1280x720+ file.
- Keep files under YouTube's thumbnail size limit where practical; simple generated graphics should stay small.
- Block city-only-small-tag thumbnails. The city name must read in a search shelf without relying on the title.
- Avoid logos, copied UI, copied thumbnails, misleading screenshots, fake platform metrics, celebrity faces, fake archival photos, shock framing, and unsupported money claims.
- Avoid tiny source tables in the final thumbnail. The source can be implied, but the main promise must be legible at small size.
- Avoid source-board/research-board thumbnails. The source board belongs inside the video; the thumbnail needs one powerful visual question.
- Do not repeat the whole title. The thumbnail should complement the title.
- The first 30 seconds of the video must show the map, source, place, or visual clue promised by the thumbnail.
- Historical images in thumbnails must have rights-ledger rows before publication. If rights are not clear, use an original map graphic, clean reconstruction, or photo-backed explanation graphic instead.
- AI-generated imagery must be visibly graphic or reconstruction-style; it must not impersonate a historical photo.
- Default creation path: OpenClaw controls strategy/source safety, then uses the Canva plugin as the preferred autonomous renderer when connected; local OpenClaw generation remains the fallback.
- Canva Free is acceptable only when the thumbnail uses user-uploaded assets/free elements and exports clean PNG/JPG with no watermark. Reject any Pro-locked element, Canva watermark, unclear Canva stock asset, or generic template feel.
- Canva Pro or Adobe Express Premium are approved paid polish options when background removal, brand templates, premium elements, or resizing materially improve quality.

## Pattern Lab Thumbnail Agent Role

The Thumbnail/Packaging agent acts as a YouTube marketer and visual editor for city-history packaging. It owns:

- thumbnail concept selection
- title-thumbnail promise match
- mobile readability
- visual hierarchy
- city/source clarity
- aesthetic polish
- advertiser-safety and copyright checks
- candidate diversity
- thumbnail learning from post-publish analytics

The agent must block a thumbnail when it is generic, overstuffed, misleading, visually low-contrast, hard to read on mobile, rights-unclear, or not clearly connected to the source proof shown in the first 30 seconds.

## Required Five-Concept / Three-Candidate Pattern

- Review concept 1: city contradiction. Make the viewer ask why the city story is not what they assumed.
- Review concept 2: map/system proof. Make the city system visible through route lines, borders, grids, or source objects.
- Review concept 3: year/time-travel. Make one date feel like a doorway into the source trail.
- Review concept 4: vanished place. Make the viewer ask what disappeared and why.
- Review concept 5: documentary fall/rise. Challenge the familiar story with a dated source, map, or photo clue.

Small variations are not enough. A valid five-concept set must test five different click hypotheses, then select the strongest three production candidates.
